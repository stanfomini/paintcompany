## 1. Colorado Mechanic's Lien Laws

**Governing Statute**: C.R.S. SS 38-22-101 through SS 38-22-133

### Who Can File

Any person who furnishes labor, materials, machinery, tools, or equipment for the construction, alteration, or repair of any structure has mechanic's lien rights. This includes painting contractors -- no specific license is required to file a lien (unlike electricians and plumbers who must be state-licensed).

### Notice of Intent to Lien (Required)

**Statute**: C.R.S. SS 38-22-109(3)

- Must be served **at least 10 full days** before filing the lien statement
- Must be served on **both** the property owner **and** the general contractor (failure to serve both voids the lien)
- Service method: Personal service OR registered/certified mail, return receipt requested
- Must include a copy of the completed lien statement that will be filed
- An affidavit of service must be filed with the lien statement at the county clerk and recorder

### Filing Deadlines

| Claimant Type | Filing Deadline |
|---|---|
| Day laborers (no materials) | **2 months** after last day of labor |
| All other claimants (GCs, subs, suppliers) | **4 months** after last day labor/materials furnished |
| Residential (1-2 family) with bona fide purchaser | **2 months** (reduced from 4) |

- Punch list items and remedial work do **not** extend these periods
- An extension can be filed with the county clerk to extend to either 4 months after project completion or 6 months after filing the extension, whichever occurs first

### Lien Statement Contents

The filed lien statement must include:
- Owner/reputed owner information (or statement if unknown)
- Claimant's name and address
- Prime contractor information (or statement if unknown)
- Hiring party information
- Property description sufficient for identification
- Amount claimed
- Affidavit proving proper service of the Notice of Intent

### Enforcement (Foreclosure) Deadline

- Must file a lawsuit to enforce the lien within **6 months** after completion or last furnishing of services/materials (whichever is later)
- If not filed within 6 months, the right to foreclose is lost

### Additional Requirements

- Lien statements must be **notarized** to be valid
- Filed at the **county recorder's office** where the property is located
- Once paid, file an Acknowledgment of Satisfaction within **10 days** of written request, or face **$10/day** penalties

### Practical Implications for Painting Contractors

A painting contractor working directly with a homeowner (as prime contractor) has full lien rights. The key is to:
1. Keep detailed records of work dates and materials furnished
2. Serve the Notice of Intent at least 10 days before filing
3. File within 4 months of last work performed
4. Sue to foreclose within 6 months if needed

## 2. Colorado Home Improvement Contract Requirements

### General Rule: No Specific Home Improvement Statute

**Colorado does not have a comprehensive "Home Improvement Act"** like some states (e.g., Connecticut, Maryland, Pennsylvania). There is no state-mandated form, no required disclosures specific to home improvement contracts, and no home improvement contractor registration at the state level for painting contractors.

### What IS Required

While there is no painting-specific statute, the following general requirements apply:

**Written Contract Best Practices** (not all statutorily mandated, but failure to provide may constitute deceptive trade practice under CCPA):
- Scope of work
- Materials to be used
- Project timeline
- Payment terms
- Change order procedures

**Roofing Contracts** (for reference -- these DO have specific requirements):
- Must include a **72-hour rescission clause** allowing cancellation with full refund
- This is specific to residential roofing and does NOT apply to painting

### Building Permits

Painting work is generally **exempt from building permits** in Colorado. Per the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department and other jurisdictions, the following do NOT require permits:
- Interior and exterior painting
- Papering, tiling, floor coverings
- Cabinets, countertops, carpeting

### Construction Defect Action Reform Act (CDARA)

**Statute**: C.R.S. SS 13-20-801 et seq.

While not a contract-formation requirement, CDARA governs how construction defect claims (including painting defects) are handled:

**Pre-Litigation Notice of Claim (NOC) Process**:
1. Claimant must send written NOC describing defects **75 days** before filing lawsuit (90 days for commercial)
2. Construction professional has **30 days** to complete inspection
3. Within **30 days** after inspection (45 for commercial), contractor may offer to settle
4. Claimant has **15 days** to accept or reject the offer
5. Failure to follow NOC process results in case being **stayed** until completed

**HB24-1230** (proposed additional protections for property owners) was **not enacted** -- it was listed as "Lost" in the 2024 session.

### Complaint Volume

The Colorado Attorney General's office received nearly **1,000 complaints** related to home improvement fraud in 2023, ranging from project abandonment to overcharging for substandard work.

## 3. Colorado Contractor Licensing

### State-Level: NO Painting License Required

**Colorado does not require a state license for painting contractors.** Only two construction trades require state licensing:
1. **Electricians** -- State license required
2. **Plumbers** -- State license required

Painting contractors can operate without a state-issued license.

### Local Requirements Vary

Many cities and counties issue their own contractor licenses. Requirements vary significantly:

| Jurisdiction | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Denver | Construction Supervisor certification may be required |
| Boulder | License required for work requiring building or right-of-way permits |
| Colorado Springs | Local business license |
| Other jurisdictions | Check local building department |

### General Business Requirements

While no painting license is needed, the following are typically required:
- **Business registration** with the Colorado Secretary of State
- **Local business license** (varies by municipality)
- **General liability insurance** (effectively required for local registration; needed when jobs exceed $200)
- **Workers' compensation insurance** (if employees are hired)
- **Sales tax license** if selling tangible goods

### Practical Note

The absence of a state painting license means there is no state exam, bond, or experience requirement. This is a **lower barrier to entry** compared to states like California (CSLB license required) or Florida (certified/registered specialty contractor).

## 4. Colorado Consumer Protection Act (CCPA)

**Governing Statute**: C.R.S. SS 6-1-101 et seq.

### Unfair or Deceptive Trade Practices

**Statute**: C.R.S. SS 6-1-105

A person engages in a deceptive trade practice when, in the course of business, they knowingly or recklessly:

1. **Pass off** goods/services as those of another
2. Make **false representations** as to source, sponsorship, approval, or certification
3. Make **false representations** as to affiliation, connection, or association
4. Use **deceptive representations** of geographic origin
5. Make **false representations** as to characteristics, ingredients, uses, benefits, alterations, or quantities of goods/services

### Application to Painting Contractors

Relevant violations for painting contractors include:
- Advertising credentials or certifications not held
- Misrepresenting paint quality, brand, or coverage
- Bait-and-switch pricing
- Making false claims about experience or past work
- Failing to disclose material facts (e.g., known defects)
- Abandoning a project after receiving payment

### Enforcement

- **Public enforcement**: Colorado Attorney General's Office
- **Private right of action**: Individual consumers can sue
- **Damages**: Greater of actual damages or statutory damages
- **Treble damages**: If defendant acted in **bad faith** (proven by clear and convincing evidence), damages can be **tripled** (3x actual damages)
- **Attorney's fees**: Prevailing plaintiff may recover reasonable attorney's fees

### Construction-Specific Provisions

- C.R.S. SS 13-20-806(7)(a) declares **void** any contractual waiver of rights under CDARA or CCPA
- Any attempt to include such waivers is itself a CCPA violation

## 5. Colorado Prompt Payment Laws

### Public Projects Only

**Governing Statute**: C.R.S. SS 24-91-103

Colorado's Prompt Payment Act applies **only to public construction projects**. It does NOT apply to private residential painting contracts.

**Public Project Requirements** (for reference):
| Party | Payment Due |
|---|---|
| Prime contractors | End of each calendar month or as soon as practicable |
| Final payment | Within 60 days of completion and acceptance |
| Subcontractors | Within 7 days after payment received from above |

**Late Payment Penalty** (public projects): Interest accrues on the 8th day at **15% per year** (1.25%/month), or the contract rate, whichever is higher.

### Private Residential Projects

**No statutory prompt payment requirement exists for private projects.** Payment terms are governed entirely by the contract. This means:

- Payment schedules must be clearly stated in your contract
- Late payment penalties must be contractually agreed upon
- Interest on late payments is subject to the general usury laws (see Section 10)
- Without a contract provision, the default statutory interest rate of **8% per annum** applies

### Best Practice for Painting Contractors

Since there is no statutory prompt payment law for residential work, the contract should clearly specify:
- Payment schedule (deposit, progress payments, final payment)
- Due dates or payment triggers (e.g., "due upon completion of each phase")
- Late payment interest rate (up to 45% per annum maximum under usury law)
- Collection costs and attorney's fees provisions

## 6. Colorado Privacy Act (CPA)

**Governing Statute**: C.R.S. SS 6-1-1301 through SS 6-1-1314

**Effective Date**: July 1, 2023

### Applicability Thresholds

The CPA applies to entities that conduct business in Colorado OR deliver commercial products/services targeted to Colorado residents AND meet **either** threshold:

| Threshold | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Threshold 1** | Control or process personal data of **100,000 or more** Colorado consumers per calendar year |
| **Threshold 2** | Control or process personal data of **at least 25,000** consumers AND derive revenue or receive a discount from the **sale** of that personal data |

**No minimum revenue threshold** -- unlike some state privacy laws, the CPA does not have a revenue floor.

### Likely Applicability to a Painting Company CRM

A typical painting contractor CRM (even with several thousand customer records) would almost certainly **fall below both thresholds**. The CPA is designed for larger businesses handling significant volumes of consumer data. However, if the CRM grows to serve multiple painting companies or aggregates data across a large platform, reassessment would be needed.

### Consumer Rights (If Applicable)

If the thresholds are met, consumers have the right to:
1. **Access** -- Know what personal data is collected
2. **Correction** -- Request correction of inaccurate data
3. **Deletion** -- Request deletion of personal data
4. **Portability** -- Obtain a portable copy of their data
5. **Opt-out** -- Opt out of: (a) targeted advertising, (b) sale of personal data, (c) profiling for decisions with legal or significant effects

### Controller Obligations

- **Privacy notice**: Clear and accessible disclosure of data practices
- **Universal opt-out**: Must honor Global Privacy Control (GPC) signals (since July 1, 2024)
- **Data minimization**: Avoid unnecessary data collection
- **Consent for sensitive data**: Must obtain consent before processing sensitive data (race, ethnicity, health, biometrics, etc.)
- **Data protection assessments**: Required for certain high-risk processing activities
- **Respond to consumer requests**: Within 45 days (one 45-day extension allowed)

### Enforcement

- **Enforced by**: Colorado Attorney General only (no private right of action)
- **Penalties**: Up to **$20,000 per violation**
- **Cure period**: 60-day right to cure expired on January 1, 2025

### Exemptions

- Employee data (HR/employment records)
- Data governed by HIPAA, GLBA, FERPA, FCRA, COPPA
- Nonprofit organizations
- Higher education institutions

## 7. UCC Applicability to Painting Contracts

### General Rule: UCC Article 2 Does NOT Apply

UCC Article 2 (C.R.S. SS 4-2-101 et seq. in Colorado) governs the **sale of goods**. Painting contractor services are predominantly **service contracts**, not goods contracts.

### The Predominant Factor Test

Colorado (like most jurisdictions) applies the **"predominant factor" test** to mixed contracts (those involving both goods and services):

**The test asks**: Is the predominant factor, thrust, and purpose of the contract:
- (a) The **rendition of service** with goods incidentally involved? (UCC does NOT apply)
- (b) A **transaction of sale** with labor incidentally involved? (UCC applies)

### Factors Considered

1. **Language of the contract** -- Does it emphasize "service," "labor," or "work" vs. "sale" or "purchase"?
2. **Nature of the business** -- Is the contractor primarily a service provider?
3. **Intrinsic worth of materials** -- What portion of the contract price is for paint vs. labor?
4. **Circumstances of the parties** -- What was the primary objective?

### Application to Painting Contracts

For a typical painting contract:
- **Labor** constitutes 60-80% of the total price
- **Paint and materials** are 20-40%
- The homeowner's primary objective is the **service** of having their home painted
- Paint is **incidental** to the service being rendered

**Conclusion**: UCC Article 2 almost certainly does **not** apply to residential painting contracts. Common law contract principles govern instead.

### Practical Implications

Because the UCC does not apply:
- No **implied warranty of merchantability** under UCC SS 2-314
- No UCC **statute of frauds** requiring writing for goods over $500
- **Common law** implied warranties (workmanship, habitability) apply instead
- **Common law** contract formation rules apply (offer, acceptance, consideration)
- The general **statute of frauds** may still require writing for contracts not performable within one year

## 8. Colorado Right to Cancel / Cooling-Off Period

### Federal FTC Rule (Baseline)

The FTC's Cooling-Off Rule (16 CFR Part 429) gives consumers **3 business days** to cancel purchases of $25 or more made at their home or at a location that is not the seller's permanent place of business.

### Colorado Home Solicitation Sale Law

**Governing Statute**: C.R.S. SS 5-3-401 through SS 5-3-405

**Definition**: A "home solicitation sale" is a consumer credit sale where:
- The seller (or agent) **personally solicits** the sale
- The buyer's agreement is given at a **residence**

**Cancellation Right**: C.R.S. SS 5-3-402

The buyer may cancel until **midnight of the third business day** after signing. This mirrors the federal rule but is codified in state law.

### How Cancellation Works

- Buyer sends **written notice** to the seller at the address in the contract
- No particular form required -- any written expression indicating intent not to be bound
- If sent by mail, cancellation is effective **when deposited** in a mailbox (properly addressed, postage prepaid)

### Emergency Exception

The buyer **cannot** cancel if ALL of the following are true:
1. Buyer provides a **separate, signed, dated statement** (not a printed form) describing an emergency
2. The emergency requires **immediate remedy** to safeguard health, safety, welfare, or prevent property damage
3. The seller **begins performance in good faith** before cancellation notice is given
4. The goods cannot be returned in substantially good condition

### What This Means for Painting Contractors

- If you solicit at the customer's door or home and they sign a contract there, they have **3 business days to cancel**
- If the customer contacts you and comes to your office to sign, this law does **not** apply
- If the customer requests an estimate at their home and signs there, this **likely applies**
- Best practice: Include a **Notice of Cancellation** form with every home-solicited contract, as required by both FTC and C.R.S. SS 5-3-403

### Colorado Does NOT Have Extended Cooling-Off

Unlike some states that provide longer cancellation periods (e.g., California's 7-day period for certain senior citizen contracts), Colorado does **not** extend the cancellation period beyond 3 business days for general home improvement contracts. The only exception is the **72-hour rescission** for residential roofing contracts, which does not apply to painting.

## 9. Colorado Warranty Laws

### Implied Warranties (Common Law)

Colorado recognizes implied warranties for construction work through common law (case law rather than statute):

**1. Implied Warranty of Workmanship**
- Established in *Carpenter v. Donohoe*, 154 Colo. 78, 388 P.2d 399 (1964)
- Requires work to be performed in a **skillful and workmanlike manner**
- Completed work must meet **industry standards** of quality and craftsmanship
- Must comply with applicable **building and structural codes**

**2. Implied Warranty of Habitability**
- Ensures the structure is **suitable for living/occupancy**
- Encompasses building code compliance, safety standards, basic amenities
- Most applicable to new home construction but can extend to significant renovations

**3. Implied Warranty of Code Compliance**
- Work must be performed according to **local building codes and laws**

### Statutory Protections

**C.R.S. SS 13-20-806(7)(a)**: Expressly **prohibits** contractual waivers or limitations of:
- Implied warranty protections
- Time periods for bringing construction defect claims
- Any provision purporting to waive these protections is **void as against public policy**

### Statutes of Limitation and Repose

| Type | Period | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **Statute of Limitations** | **2 years** | From discovery (or when homeowner should have discovered) the defect |
| **Statute of Repose** | **6 years** | From substantial completion of the improvement |
| **Extension** | Up to **2 additional years** | If defect is discovered in years 5 or 6, bringing total to up to 8 years |

### Application to Painting Work

Painting defects (peeling, flaking, improper prep, wrong product used, color mismatch) would fall under the implied warranty of workmanship. Key considerations:

- Paint failures typically manifest within **1-3 years**, well within the statute of limitations
- The **2-year discovery rule** starts when the homeowner notices (or should notice) the defect
- A contractor cannot contractually shorten these periods
- Express warranties (e.g., "5-year warranty on all work") can supplement but not replace implied warranties

## 10. Colorado Interest Rate Limits / Usury Laws

**Governing Statutes**: C.R.S. SS 5-12-101 through SS 5-12-103

### Statutory Rates

| Type | Rate | Statute |
|---|---|---|
| **Default rate** (no agreement) | **8% per annum**, compounded annually | C.R.S. SS 5-12-101 |
| **Maximum contractual rate** | **45% per annum** | C.R.S. SS 5-12-103 |
| **Criminal usury** | Exceeding **45%** per annum | C.R.S. SS 18-15-104 |

### Key Rules

**C.R.S. SS 5-12-101**: If there is no agreement or provision of law for a different rate, interest on money shall be at **8% per annum**, compounded annually.

**C.R.S. SS 5-12-102**: Statutory interest rate for judgments and other obligations.

**C.R.S. SS 5-12-103**: Parties may stipulate for payment of a greater rate than 8% per annum, **but not exceeding 45% per annum**. An increased rate contingent upon nonpayment (i.e., a "default rate") is permissible within this cap.

### Practical Application for Painting Contractors

For painting contract payment terms:

| Scenario | Applicable Rate |
|---|---|
| No interest clause in contract | **8% per annum** default |
| Contract specifies interest on late payment | Up to **45% per annum** maximum |
| Typical "reasonable" late payment rate | **1.5% per month** (18% per annum) is common |
| Court judgment on unpaid amount | Statutory rate applies |

### Recommendations

- **Always include** an interest clause in the contract for past-due amounts
- **1.5% per month** (18% annually) is standard in the industry and well within Colorado's limits
- Specify whether interest compounds or is simple
- Include language about who pays collection costs and attorney's fees

Based on the above research, a Colorado painting contract should include:
